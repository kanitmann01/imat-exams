# GK LEANING: what the real IMAT papers actually weight (2024, 2025)

Prepared 20 Sep 2026 for a new 60 question medium-hard GK bank (exam 29 Sep 2026).
Sources: IMAT_2024_official.pdf, IMAT_2025_official.pdf (both fully text extractable, 11 pages, 60 questions each), syllabus Annex A section 1 bullet, GK_Drill_100.json.

The 2 real GK items, one line each:
- 2024 Q1, literature: single-fact recall, author of the novel "To the Lighthouse"; five options, all female authors of the same era and language (key: Virginia Woolf).
- 2024 Q3, European/world history: single-fact recall, which two kingdoms fought the Hundred Years' War; five options, each a pair of European kingdoms.

Irony to note: the syllabus bullet names institutions, legal, economic and citizenship phenomena, yet these two papers contain zero items on any of them.

## 2. Lean summary

- GK is marginal by design: 2 of 60 in 2024, 0 of 60 in 2025 (2 of 120 overall, under 2 percent). Section 1 is 4 items and in 2025 it was 100 percent reading comprehension.
- Observed buckets: only world literature and European/world history. Nothing on Italian history, constitution, EU, UN, economics, geography, religion, science history, or current affairs.
- Dated vs evergreen: 100 percent evergreen. No current-affairs, no living-officeholder, no dated-number items anywhere in either paper. Dated facts have low expected value at the margin.
- Forms: only single-fact recall with short options. No matching, no "which happened first", no pick-the-true-statement (that long-claim form appears in science and reading items, not GK).
- Practical read: a 60 item bank covers far more GK than the real paper demands; treat GK as a small insurance section of canonical, quick facts with plausible distractors, not obscurity.

## 3. Style rules distilled from the real items

- One fact, one line: stem is a short direct question, no passage, no multi-clause setup.
- All five options are the same grammatical category and near equal length (five author names, five kingdom pairs); never mix names with statements.
- Distractors are canonical same-family names the candidate can conflate (same nationality, era or type), not absurd fillers; no "none of the above".
- Scope is school-book canonical (the famous novel, the famous war); difficulty comes from distractor plausibility, not from obscure detail.
- Evergreen wording only: no "last year", no living officeholders, no statistics that expire.
- One unambiguous key; keep negation stems ("CANNOT", "NOT correct") rare in GK, the real GK items are all positive stems.

## 4. Recommended split for the new 60 question bank (medium-hard)

Weighted by the observed lean (evergreen canonical humanities and world tilt), syllabus bullet coverage, and avoidance of GK_Drill_100's heavy topics.

- A. European and world history (non Italian): 10
- B. World literature, authors and works: 8
- C. International institutions (UN system, WHO, NATO, ICC, Red Cross): 8
- D. Economics, finance and citizenship basics: 8
- E. Italian institutions and constitutional law, hard angles (revision procedure, EU law relations, electoral law): 6
- F. History of science, world scientists (outside the Italian canon): 7
- G. Geography, world and Italy: 6
- H. Philosophy, religion and society: 5
- I. Recent settled events, 2025 to 2026: 2
- Total: 60

GK_Drill_100 saturation (tags per macro-group, out of 100): EU 16, civics/constitution 16, Italian history 14, art 12, Italian letters 11, Italian scientists 10, current affairs 10, reading skills 8, ideas/philosophy 3. Saturated and to avoid re-drilling: EU treaties/euro/institutions, Italian civics (Court, Parliament, government), Risorgimento-to-Republic history, Italian art and letters canon, Italian scientists (Volta, Fermi, Marconi), recent Nobels/papacy/sport. Under-covered by the drill: world history outside Italy, world literature, geography (0), economics (0), non-EU institutions (0).
